*Under Sand* is a tabletop roleplaying game set beneath the surface — in the tunnels, caverns, and buried ruins that wind endlessly below a scorched world above.

## Overview

Players take on the roles of Delvers: scavengers, scholars, and survivors who make their living crawling through the underworld in search of relics, refuge, and answers. The world above is hostile. Below, it is dark, dangerous, and full of things that have never seen the sun.

*Under Sand* uses a custom dice system built around resource scarcity and collaborative risk management. Every descent is a negotiation between what you need and what you can afford to lose.

---

## Core Rules

### The Dice

*Under Sand* uses pools of six-sided dice (d6). Players assemble a pool based on their character's relevant **Trait** and **Gear**, then roll.

- **4–6**: Success
- **1–3**: Failure

Count successes. The Conductor (GM) sets the difficulty as a number of successes required.

### Traits

Every Delver has four Traits, each rated from 1 to 4 (number of dice in the pool):

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| **Grit** | Physical endurance, brute force, and pain tolerance |
| **Wit** | Quick thinking, memory, and pattern recognition |
| **Reach** | Agility, dexterity, and spatial awareness |
| **Read** | Perception, intuition, and reading other creatures |

### Gear Dice

Relevant tools and equipment add bonus dice to a roll. Gear is tracked by **Condition**:

- **Intact** (+2 dice): Full functionality
- **Worn** (+1 die): Functional but showing strain
- **Broken** (0 dice): Must be repaired before use

When you roll a pool entirely of 1s (a **Total Failure**), your gear degrades by one step.

## The Delve

### Planning

Before descending, players collectively choose their **Depth**, **Load**, and **Purpose**.

- **Depth** sets how far down they go (1–5). Higher depth means greater rewards and greater danger.
- **Load** determines how much gear each Delver can carry. Heavier loads limit **Reach** rolls.
- **Purpose** is why they're going: Extraction, Exploration, or Evasion.

### The Clock

Each Delve uses a shared **Air Clock** — a countdown representing breathable air, torch fuel, and the patience of whatever lives down there. The clock starts at 6 and ticks down on bad rolls, special events, and whenever players take time to rest. When it hits 0, the Delvers must surface or face consequences.

### Hazards

The Conductor presents hazards as **Threats**. Each Threat has:

- A **Name** (what it is)
- A **Trigger** (what sets it off)
- A **Cost** (what happens on failure)
- A **Clock** (how many rounds before it escalates)

Threats are not always hostile creatures. Collapsed tunnels, flooded chambers, toxic air pockets, and rival Delvers are all Threats.

## Delvers

### Character Creation

1. **Choose a Background** — Where did you come from before the sand took everything?
2. **Assign Trait Points** — Distribute 8 points across your four Traits (max 4 per Trait, min 1)
3. **Select Starting Gear** — Choose 3 items from the Gear List
4. **Name Your Scars** — Choose 2 Scars that reflect past Delves gone wrong (see Scars, below)

### Scars

Scars are permanent marks left by dangerous Delves. Each Scar has a **Burden** (a narrative complication) and a **Boon** (a mechanical advantage).

**Example Scars:**

| Scar | Burden | Boon |
|---|---|---|
| *Earless* | Cannot benefit from audio-based clues | Add 1 die to **Read** when in total silence |
| *Cracked Lens* | **Wit** rolls to read text cost 1 extra success | Add 2 dice to **Wit** when recalling memorized information |
| *Tunnel Lungs* | -1 to starting Air Clock | Ignore the first Air Clock tick per session |
| *Marked* | Certain creatures will always target you first | Creatures that see you must make a Threat roll or hesitate |

## The Conductor

### Running Threats

When a Threat triggers, narrate the situation and ask the players what they do. Only roll dice when:
- The outcome is uncertain
- Both success and failure are interesting

### Conductor Moves

When players fail a roll or look to you to see what happens next, make a **Conductor Move**:

- **Reveal a hidden cost** — something they thought was safe, isn't
- **Tick the Air Clock** — time is running out
- **Escalate a Threat** — something gets worse
- **Introduce a new Threat** — the environment pushes back
- **Offer a hard bargain** — they can succeed, but at a price
